Cronometer vs MyFitnessPal in 2026

Quick verdict: MyFitnessPal is faster and easier for everyday calorie counting with the biggest food database. Cronometer is the choice for micronutrient tracking, data accuracy, and athletes who care about more than calories. When Cronometer wins

  • You track iron, B12, magnesium, omega-3, or other micronutrients seriously.
  • You want fewer user-submitted entries with wrong calorie counts.
  • You follow a specific diet (keto, vegan) and need detailed nutrient breakdowns.
  • You prefer a lower-cost premium tier (~$10/mo vs ~$20/mo).

When MyFitnessPal wins

  • You log restaurant meals, ethnic foods, and branded products quickly.
  • Social features and large community recipe imports matter to you.
  • You want the broadest third-party integrations and wearable sync.
  • Calories and macros are enough — you do not need 84 nutrient columns.

FAQ

Is Cronometer more accurate than MyFitnessPal?

Generally yes for micronutrients. Cronometer pulls from verified lab-analyzed databases (NCCDB, USDA). MyFitnessPal's crowd-sourced entries are convenient but sometimes duplicate or incorrect. For calories alone, both work if you verify packaged labels.

Can I import MyFitnessPal data into Cronometer?

Cronometer supports importing CSV exports from MyFitnessPal for foods and biometrics. Migration takes some cleanup because database entries do not map one-to-one.

Which is better for keto or bodybuilding?

Cronometer for tracking net carbs, electrolytes, and amino acids. MyFitnessPal for speed when you already know your meal plan. Use VitalAI's Protein Calculator to set intake targets for either app.

Which has the better free version?

Both free tiers are solid. Cronometer free includes full micronutrient tracking; Gold adds barcode scan and no ads. MyFitnessPal free covers basic logging but limits macros and removes ads only on Premium.
