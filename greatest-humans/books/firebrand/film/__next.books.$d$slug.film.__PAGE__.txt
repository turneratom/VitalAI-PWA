### Screenplay by Brad Turner

---

## ACT ONE

FADE IN:

### INT. GREENWICH VILLAGE HOUSE — BEDCHAMBER — PRE-DAWN — 1809

A candle gutters beside a chaos of papers.

THOMAS PAINE, 72, lies swollen and nearly motionless. His famous eyes remain alive. MARGUERITE DE BONNEVILLE, 42, holds a cup to his lips while his shaking hand pretends to hold it alone.

Outside: wagon wheels, bells, a city beginning without him.

A FLY lands on a stained copy of *COMMON SENSE*.

Its legs SCRAPE the paper.

MATCH CUT TO:

### INT. PAINE WORKSHOP, THETFORD — DAY — 1748

A steel tool SCRAPES whalebone.

JOSEPH PAINE, a Quaker stay-maker, works beneath a window powdered with dust. His eight-year-old son THOMAS watches from under the bench.

JOSEPH

Hand me the awl, Tom.

The boy studies the tools.

YOUNG THOMAS

Why do they call you cordwainer if you make stays?

JOSEPH

Because men keep old words after the work has changed.

YOUNG THOMAS

Why?

JOSEPH

Habit.

Joseph takes the awl.

JOSEPH (CONT’D)

Mind that question. It gets a man in trouble.

Through the shop window, a wealthy CUSTOMER waits while Joseph bows over her order. She does not look at him.

CUSTOMER

Friday. Not one day later.

YOUNG THOMAS

He said Saturday.

The woman finally sees the boy.

CUSTOMER

Children should not interrupt their betters.

YOUNG THOMAS

How do we know you are?

Joseph grips Thomas’s shoulder before the answer becomes disaster.

### EXT. THETFORD MARKET — DAY

A CROWD surrounds a scaffold. A thin MAN stands beneath a rope.

Young Thomas slips between adults. The condemned man’s crime is read: theft.

The trap opens.

Thomas flinches at the sound.

Nearby, a BAKER covers his bread against the dust.

### EXT. RIVER PATH — DUSK

Joseph finds Thomas throwing stones hard enough to hurt water.

YOUNG THOMAS

They killed him for taking food.

JOSEPH

He stole.

YOUNG THOMAS

Was he hungry?

Joseph cannot answer without indicting the world.

JOSEPH

The world is hard. We do not harden with it.

YOUNG THOMAS

Then what do we do?

Joseph considers the furious child.

JOSEPH

Begin with the truth.

### MONTAGE — THOMAS FAILS FORWARD

— TEENAGE THOMAS stitches stays, eyes fixed on a book hidden behind a customer’s pattern.

— He boards a PRIVATEER. Joseph hauls him back to shore before sailing.

— In London, Thomas searches a nearly empty purse.

— He marries MARY LAMBERT. Months later, a shrouded shape is carried from their room. Thomas stands alone.

— He teaches schoolboys who weaponize their ink.

— In uniform as an EXCISE OFFICER, Thomas measures casks while a merchant slips coins toward him. Thomas pushes them back.

— He marries ELIZABETH OLLIVE. They run a shop. Bills multiply. Their silences do too.

— Thomas drafts a petition by candlelight: better pay for excise officers.

### INT. LONDON COFFEEHOUSE — NIGHT — 1774

Smoke, pamphlets, men performing certainty.

THOMAS PAINE, now 37, rough-edged and intense, argues with a GOVERNMENT CLERK.

PAINE

You pay an officer less than survival, then act astonished when he sells honesty to the highest bidder.

CLERK

You were dismissed for neglect.

PAINE

I was dismissed because neglect is cheaper than reform.

A gentle laugh from BENJAMIN FRANKLIN, 68, spectacles reflecting candlelight.

FRANKLIN

That sentence is better than your petition.

PAINE

The petition was required to be polite.

FRANKLIN

There is its defect.

Later. The room has thinned. Franklin reads Paine’s pages.

FRANKLIN (CONT’D)

You have three professions here. Statistics, fury, and spelling.

PAINE

Which pays?

FRANKLIN

In England? Spelling.

Paine smiles despite himself.

FRANKLIN (CONT’D)

The colonies need men who can explain a grievance without dressing it for court.

PAINE

I know nothing of America.

FRANKLIN

An advantage. Americans know so much of it they cannot see it.

He writes a letter of introduction.

FRANKLIN (CONT’D)

Go before England finds another use for you.

### EXT. ATLANTIC — THE SHIP *LONDON PACKET* — NIGHT

Atlantic gray. The ship climbs a black wall of water.

Below deck, Paine burns with fever among coughing passengers. A SAILOR pours water between his lips.

SAILOR

Philadelphia tomorrow, if the wind is Christian.

PAINE

Wind has no religion.

He vomits into a bucket.

### EXT. PHILADELPHIA WHARF — DAY — NOVEMBER 1774

Paine is carried ashore in a blanket.

His blurred view: masts, Black dockworkers, merchants, pigs, brick, sky.

FRANKLIN (V.O.)

The bearer, Mr. Thomas Paine, is an ingenious, worthy young man.

### INT. ROBERT AITKEN’S PRINT SHOP — DAY

Metal type. Wet paper. Ink-black hands.

ROBERT AITKEN, brisk and skeptical, looks Paine over.

AITKEN

Franklin calls every stray dog ingenious.

PAINE

Only the ones that bite.

Aitken hands him a proof.

AITKEN

Correct it.

Paine scans.

PAINE

The argument or the commas?

AITKEN

The commas.

Paine marks both.

### INT. AITKEN’S PRINT SHOP — LATER

Paine edits the *PENNSYLVANIA MAGAZINE*. A page condemning the slave trade sits in type.

Aitken reads uneasily.

AITKEN

Subscribers dislike instruction at breakfast.

PAINE

Then print it at supper.

AITKEN

You have been in America six months.

PAINE

Long enough to hear men demand liberty with servants standing behind them.

Outside, bells CLANG.

A BOY races in.

BOY

Shots in Massachusetts! Regulars and militia!

The shop empties into the street.

### INT. PHILADELPHIA TAVERN — NIGHT

The room boils over Lexington and Concord. JOHN DICKINSON argues for restraint. Merchants and mechanics crowd close.

DICKINSON

We seek our rights as Englishmen, not separation from England.

PAINE

If your rights require another man’s permission, they are favors.

DICKINSON

And you are?

PAINE

New enough to ask the impolite question.

Scattered laughter.

PAINE (CONT’D)

Why should a continent be governed by an island?

MERCHANT

Because it always has.

PAINE

That is not a reason. It is the absence of one grown old.

Silence spreads outward.

### EXT. PHILADELPHIA STREET — NIGHT

DR. BENJAMIN RUSH catches up with Paine.

RUSH

Write it.

PAINE

Write what?

RUSH

What that room is now afraid it thinks.

PAINE

It would be treason.

RUSH

Not if we win.

### INT. RENTED ROOM — NIGHT

Candle. Ink. Cold.

Paine attacks a page. Writes, crosses out, speaks to empty chairs.

PAINE

“Society is produced by our wants...”

No.

He begins again.

PAINE (CONT’D)

“Society in every state is a blessing...”

He writes faster.

A KNOCK. Rush enters, reads over his shoulder.

RUSH

You cannot call it *Plain Truth*. Another man has used it.

PAINE

Then what is rarer than plain truth?

Rush scans a passage.

RUSH

Common sense.

They look at each other.

### INT. ROBERT BELL’S PRINT SHOP — NIGHT

Pressmen pull sheets. ROBERT BELL examines the title:

*COMMON SENSE. Addressed to the Inhabitants of America.*

No author’s name.

BELL

If they hang someone, I prefer they begin with the man who supplied the words.

PAINE

If they hang us, sales will suffer.

The press SLAMS.

### MONTAGE — THE PAMPHLET MOVES

The same pages pass through radically different hands.

— A BOOKSELLER sells the first copy.

— A MERCHANT reads aloud at a coffeehouse.

MERCHANT

“The cause of America is in a great measure the cause of all mankind.”

— A FARM WOMAN reads while kneading dough.

FARM WOMAN

“We have it in our power to begin the world over again.”

— A BLACK SAILOR reads silently, then looks toward the State House.

— A RIDER stuffs bundles into saddlebags.

— A MINISTER quotes it after prayer.

— A LOYALIST throws it into a fire. His son rescues another copy from a desk.

— In a militia camp, a SOLDIER with no letters listens.

SOLDIER

Read that part again.

— Press after press joins the rhythm until the sound becomes MUSKET FIRE.

### INT. CONTINENTAL CONGRESS — CORRIDOR — DAY

Delegates surge past Paine without knowing him.

Rush approaches, barely containing wonder.

RUSH

They say a hundred thousand.

PAINE

Printers say whatever improves an edition.

RUSH

Congress is speaking openly of independence.

PAINE

Good.

RUSH

Is that all?

PAINE

No. Now they must do it.

### EXT. STATE HOUSE YARD — DAY — JULY 1776

Bells. Crowds. The Declaration is read.

Paine stands at the edge, anonymous.

An OLD LABORER cups a hand to his ear.

OLD LABORER

What did he say?

A GIRL, 12, has a printed broadside.

GIRL

“All men are created equal.”

Near her, the Black sailor from the montage listens.

OLD LABORER

Read it again.

She does.

Paine sees the claim travel farther than the men who signed it intended.

CUT TO BLACK.

## ACT TWO

### EXT. NEW JERSEY ROAD — NIGHT — DECEMBER 1776

An army retreats through snow.

Paine walks beside the soldiers, a blanket around his shoulders, musket on his back. Men leave dark blood where their shoes have failed.

A YOUNG SOLDIER stumbles. Paine catches him.

YOUNG SOLDIER

Is this what beginning the world looks like?

PAINE

The old one objects.

Ahead, wagons vanish into white.

### INT. NEWARK TAVERN — NIGHT

Paine writes beside a dying fire while exhausted soldiers sleep sitting up.

The Young Soldier wakes.

YOUNG SOLDIER

Who are you writing to?

PAINE

You.

YOUNG SOLDIER

I am here.

PAINE

Tomorrow you may not be.

Paine writes:

PAINE (V.O.)

These are the times that try men’s souls.

### EXT. CONTINENTAL CAMP — EVENING

An OFFICER reads *THE AMERICAN CRISIS* to ranks of hollow-eyed men. GENERAL GEORGE WASHINGTON watches from horseback.

OFFICER

“The summer soldier and the sunshine patriot will, in this crisis, shrink from the service of their country...”

The phrase passes over the men.

The Young Soldier straightens.

### EXT. DELAWARE RIVER — NIGHT

Boats push through ice.

Paine grips the side as soldiers cross toward Trenton. Snow swallows the far bank.

Washington’s horse steps into freezing water.

No painting. No pose. Only men trying not to drown before battle.

### INT. PHILADELPHIA GOVERNMENT OFFICE — DAY — 1779

Victory is not complete, but bureaucracy has arrived.

Paine, now secretary to Congress’s foreign affairs committee, slaps documents before TWO CONGRESSMEN.

PAINE

The French supplied these goods as aid. Certain men invoice them as private merchandise and keep the difference.

CONGRESSMAN

You have disclosed confidential negotiations.

PAINE

I disclosed theft.

CONGRESSMAN

You have embarrassed Congress.

PAINE

Then Congress should stop behaving embarrassingly.

The second Congressman shuts the file.

SECOND CONGRESSMAN

Your resignation will be accepted.

Paine realizes the price one beat too late.

### EXT. PHILADELPHIA STREET — DAY

Paine exits carrying a small box of papers.

The Young Soldier, older now and missing an arm, recognizes him.

ONE-ARMED VETERAN

Mr. Paine?

Paine braces for complaint.

ONE-ARMED VETERAN (CONT’D)

Trenton.

The veteran offers his remaining hand. Paine sets down the box and takes it.

### MONTAGE — PEACE AND RESTLESSNESS

— Yorktown guns surrender their smoke.

— Paine signs over proceeds, refuses profit, argues over accounts.

— The Pennsylvania Assembly votes him money.

— New York grants him a Loyalist farm at New Rochelle.

— At the farm, he stares at a broken fence, baffled.

— On paper, an elegant IRON BRIDGE rises in ink.

### INT. PARIS SALON — NIGHT — 1787

Franklin, older and frail, studies Paine’s bridge model.

FRANKLIN

One arch. No piers.

PAINE

Iron distributes the burden.

FRANKLIN

A republican bridge.

PAINE

Stone is a monarchist. It puts all its faith in mass.

Franklin laughs, then coughs deeply.

FRANKLIN

You have become famous, Tom.

PAINE

Fame is the shadow of useful work.

FRANKLIN

Shadows grow larger near sunset.

Their eyes meet. A farewell neither names.

### INT. LONDON DINING ROOM — NIGHT — 1791

EDMUND BURKE’S *REFLECTIONS ON THE REVOLUTION IN FRANCE* lies open.

Paine paces before a table of reformers, including WILLIAM GODWIN and MARY WOLLSTONECRAFT.

PAINE

He pities the plumage and forgets the dying bird.

WOLLSTONECRAFT

Then answer him.

PAINE

Everyone answers Burke.

WOLLSTONECRAFT

Not everyone has overthrown a king before breakfast.

PAINE

The Americans overthrew him. I supplied punctuation.

She pushes paper toward him.

WOLLSTONECRAFT

Supply more.

### INT. LONDON ROOM — SERIES OF NIGHTS

Paine writes *RIGHTS OF MAN*.

PAINE (V.O.)

Every age and generation must be as free to act for itself, in all cases, as the ages and generations which preceded it.

Pages accumulate.

PAINE (V.O.) (CONT’D)

Man has no property in man; neither has any generation a property in the generations which are to follow.

He drafts pensions for the aged, grants for the young, education for children.

PAINE

Rights that stop at hunger are decorations.

### MONTAGE — BRITAIN READS AND REACTS

— WORKERS pool coins for a copy.

— A SHOEMAKER reads aloud in a workshop.

— A government SPY writes names in a corner.

— A magistrate orders copies seized.

— Burke’s elegant volume sits beside Paine’s cheap, battered pamphlet.

— Prime Minister WILLIAM PITT reads a report, expression cold.

PITT

Prosecute.

### INT. DOVER INN — NIGHT — SEPTEMBER 1792

THOMAS “CLIO” RICKMAN bursts into Paine’s room.

RICKMAN

Warrant for sedition. They are searching your London rooms.

Paine keeps packing manuscripts.

RICKMAN (CONT’D)

Leave the papers.

PAINE

The papers are why I must leave.

### EXT. DOVER DOCK — DAWN

Officers hurry down the quay.

Paine’s packet pulls away moments before they arrive.

An OFFICER shouts across widening water.

OFFICER

Thomas Paine! By authority of the Crown—

PAINE

That is the authority in dispute!

Wind tears the rest away.

### EXT. CALAIS HARBOR — DAY

A jubilant French CROWD surrounds Paine. Tricolor cockades. Kisses. Shouts he does not understand.

A TRANSLATOR fights through.

TRANSLATOR

Citizen Paine, you have been elected to the National Convention.

PAINE

By whom?

TRANSLATOR

Calais.

PAINE

I have been here four minutes.

TRANSLATOR

Your reputation arrived first.

They place a tricolor sash across his chest.

### INT. NATIONAL CONVENTION, PARIS — DAY — JANUARY 1793

French stone. Packed galleries. Deputies roar over the fate of LOUIS XVI.

Paine rises, manuscript trembling slightly. The Translator stands beside him.

ROBESPIERRE watches. MARAT glares.

PAINE

I voted to abolish the monarchy.

The Translator repeats in French.

PAINE (CONT’D)

The man before us is now our prisoner. Banish him. Do not kill him.

Boos.

MARAT

(in French)

The American pities a tyrant!

TRANSLATOR

He says—

PAINE

I know what he says.

Paine faces the chamber.

PAINE (CONT’D)

If rights belong only to men we admire, they are not rights.

The Translator struggles to be heard.

PAINE (CONT’D)

You cannot teach a nation to stop kneeling by making it gather around a scaffold.

Some applaud. More howl.

Robespierre’s face gives nothing.

### EXT. PLACE DE LA RÉVOLUTION — DAY

The guillotine falls.

Paine, far back in the crowd, hears the CHEER before he sees anything.

Blood runs between paving stones and dilutes in rain.

### INT. PARIS ROOMS — NIGHT

Paine writes frantically. Outside, boots pound stairs in neighboring buildings.

On the title page: *THE AGE OF REASON*.

NICOLAS DE BONNEVILLE reads pages while MARGUERITE, younger, watches the window.

NICOLAS

You choose now to attack the churches?

PAINE

I defend God from them.

MARGUERITE

The distinction may be lost on the Committee.

PAINE

Then the Committee should learn to read.

A distant door SMASHES open.

NICOLAS

Finish quickly.

PAINE

I am trying to finish before they do.

### INT. PARIS STREET / PRISON CART — NIGHT

Guards arrest Paine. He clutches the manuscript inside his coat.

Paine spots JOEL BARLOW in the crowd and shoves pages into his hands.

PAINE

Print this.

BARLOW

Tom—

PAINE

Print it before they bury both of us.

The cart jerks forward.

### INT. LUXEMBOURG PRISON — CELL — DAY

Bare French stone. Paine shares the cell with other prisoners.

A prisoner listens as Paine explains deism.

PRISONER

You are writing theology here?

PAINE

They have removed the distraction of liberty.

PRISONER

Do you believe God will save you?

PAINE

I believe a God who constructed Saturn has better work than altering French lists.

Outside, a cart rattles away.

### INT. LUXEMBOURG PRISON — CELL — NIGHT

Paine shakes with fever. A CELL MATE props the door open for air.

A GUARD moves down the corridor marking condemned doors with CHALK.

At Paine’s open door, he makes the fatal mark on the side facing the hall.

Later, the cell mate closes the door.

The mark turns inward.

### INT. LUXEMBOURG PRISON — CORRIDOR — DAWN

Guards read the marks from the corridor. They pass Paine’s unmarked door.

Inside, Paine lies unconscious as condemned prisoners are removed nearby.

The wheels of the death carts fade.

### INT. LUXEMBOURG PRISON — MONTHS LATER

The door opens.

JAMES MONROE, the new American minister, stands with MRS. ELIZABETH MONROE.

MONROE

Mr. Paine. You are free.

Paine tries to rise and cannot.

PAINE

I was an American before you were an ambassador.

MONROE

I know.

PAINE

Washington did not.

Elizabeth Monroe kneels and wraps Paine in her own cloak.

### INT. MONROE RESIDENCE — BEDROOM — DAY

Sun. Clean linen. Paine recuperates.

Washington’s portrait hangs on a wall.

Paine dictates to a SCRIBE.

PAINE

“And as to you, sir, treacherous in private friendship and a hypocrite in public life—”

MONROE

Tom. Stop.

PAINE

He left me to die.

MONROE

We do not know what reached him.

PAINE

Power always misplaces the letter that requires courage.

MONROE

Do not let the prison write this.

Paine stares at the portrait.

PAINE

The prison earned a paragraph.

### INT. PARIS STUDY — DAY — 1798

NAPOLEON BONAPARTE circles Paine’s iron bridge model.

NAPOLEON

A statue of gold should be erected to you in every city in the universe.

PAINE

Begin with one bridge that stands.

Napoleon smiles.

NAPOLEON

Carry *Rights of Man* in your pocket when we land in England. The people will rise.

PAINE

People do not become free because a foreign army tells them they have risen.

NAPOLEON

You distrust generals.

PAINE

Only when they begin speaking of statues.

Napoleon’s smile disappears.

### INT. PAINE’S PARIS ROOM — NIGHT — 1801

Older, heavier, Paine opens a letter. Jefferson’s voice accompanies it.

JEFFERSON (V.O.)

You expressed a wish to get a passage to this country in a public vessel. Mr. Dawson is charged with orders to receive and accommodate you back to America.

Paine goes to the window. Paris below is under military order.

JEFFERSON (V.O.) (CONT’D)

I am in hopes you will find us returned generally to sentiments worthy of former times.

Paine folds the letter carefully.

MARGUERITE

Do you believe him?

PAINE

Belief is not required for passage.

### EXT. SHIP *LONDON* — ATLANTIC — DAY — 1802

Paine stands at the rail. France thins behind him.

A YOUNG AMERICAN CLERK approaches.

CLERK

Mr. Paine? My father read *Common Sense* to the whole village.

PAINE

Your father had patient neighbors.

CLERK

He says you saved the Revolution.

PAINE

The Revolution was crowded. No one saved it alone.

The clerk hesitates.

CLERK

Did you write that the Bible is false?

Paine looks west.

PAINE

I wrote that another man’s revelation is hearsay to me.

CLERK

Why attack what consoles people?

PAINE

Because a lie that consoles today may command a hanging tomorrow.

The clerk backs away, disappointed to find the whole man.

## ACT THREE

### EXT. BALTIMORE WHARF — DAY — OCTOBER 1802

The gangplank drops.

A few REPUBLICANS cheer Paine. Across the street, a MINISTER watches.

MINISTER

There goes the infidel.

An OLD DOCKWORKER removes his hat.

DOCKWORKER

There goes Mr. Common Sense.

Paine steps onto American soil between the two names.

### INT. PRESIDENT’S HOUSE, WASHINGTON — DINING ROOM — NIGHT

The capital beyond the windows is mud and darkness.

President THOMAS JEFFERSON welcomes Paine with both hands.

JEFFERSON

You took your time.

PAINE

France detained me.

They embrace.

At dinner, they speak quickly, old minds delighted by friction.

JEFFERSON

Representative government has survived its first transfer of party.

PAINE

A government congratulating itself is like a man reviewing his own trial.

JEFFERSON

You have not changed.

PAINE

I have worsened in several useful respects.

An ENSLAVED SERVANT pours wine.

Paine watches his hand, then Jefferson.

PAINE (CONT’D)

Has he consented to the government?

Jefferson’s warmth cools.

JEFFERSON

This is not a simple matter.

PAINE

No chain is simple to the man wearing it.

The servant moves away, face disciplined into absence.

JEFFERSON

Institutions yield slowly.

PAINE

Men in chains do not experience slowness as philosophy.

The friends sit inside the distance between declaration and life.

### INT. FEDERALIST NEWSPAPER OFFICE — NIGHT

An EDITOR dictates while a caricaturist draws Paine with a bottle and devil’s tail.

EDITOR

“President Jefferson welcomes to his table the loathsome apostle of French atheism—”

CARICATURIST

Bigger nose?

EDITOR

And dirtier coat.

The press begins.

### MONTAGE — AMERICA RECEIVES HIM

— Headlines: “THE INFIDEL PAINE.” “JEFFERSON’S GUEST.” “A MONSTER RETURNED.”

— Paine marks errors in the margins.

— A former friend spots him, turns, crosses the street.

— Boys trail him, shouting “Tom Paine!” as an insult.

— A tavern keeper removes a Bible from a table after Paine sits.

— A printer quietly shakes his hand.

### INT. PHILADELPHIA TAVERN — DAY

Paine eats alone.

The One-Armed Veteran, now elderly, approaches carrying a small stitched pamphlet.

ONE-ARMED VETERAN

You may not remember me.

PAINE

Trenton.

The veteran smiles, relieved.

He places the smoke-stained copy of *THE CRISIS* on the table.

ONE-ARMED VETERAN

My brother carried it at Valley Forge. He did not come out.

Paine opens the fragile pages.

ONE-ARMED VETERAN (CONT’D)

I wanted you to see what your words looked like after the war.

The pages are stained beyond diagnosis.

PAINE

Keep him with you.

ONE-ARMED VETERAN

I came to thank you.

PAINE

Then thank him for me.

The veteran takes the pamphlet. He lingers.

ONE-ARMED VETERAN

They say you deny our Savior.

Paine absorbs the inevitable second meeting.

PAINE

They say more than they read.

The veteran leaves, confused. Paine’s hand remains extended over the empty table.

### EXT. NEW ROCHELLE FARM — DAY

Wind in an orchard. Long Island Sound, a blue-gray blade in the distance.

Paine walks the farm with Marguerite and her sons BENJAMIN and THOMAS BONNEVILLE.

BENJAMIN

Did New York give you all of it?

PAINE

For services rendered.

THOMAS BONNEVILLE

What services?

PAINE

Annoying the British.

A fence rail falls.

MARGUERITE

The British have had their revenge.

### INT. NEW ROCHELLE COTTAGE — NIGHT

Paine demonstrates a bridge model to the boys.

PAINE

Every part bears weight. The trick is not to make one part bear all of it.

BENJAMIN

Like democracy?

PAINE

Democracy is more likely to steal the materials.

The boys laugh.

Marguerite watches Paine laugh with them. For a moment, he is home.

### INT. NEW ROCHELLE COTTAGE — LATER

The boys sleep. Branches scrape the roof.

Paine and Marguerite sort hostile newspapers.

MARGUERITE

Do not read that one.

PAINE

Why?

MARGUERITE

It says you are drunk, filthy, faithless, and corrupt.

PAINE

Any errors?

MARGUERITE

They have the date wrong.

He laughs, then his face tightens with gout.

Marguerite kneels to remove his shoe.

PAINE

Leave it.

MARGUERITE

Your foot is swollen.

PAINE

It is my foot.

MARGUERITE

Then command it to remove its own shoe.

He surrenders the foot, not the argument.

### EXT. NEW ROCHELLE POLLING PLACE — DAY — 1806

Men line up to vote.

Paine reaches the table. ELECTION INSPECTOR ELISHA WARD checks the register.

WARD

Your vote cannot be received.

PAINE

My name is there.

WARD

You became a citizen of France when elected to their Convention.

PAINE

France made me a representative. It did not unmake me an American.

WARD

The inspectors have decided.

PAINE

I came here in 1774. I wrote for independence before half this room would say the word aloud.

Men look away.

WARD

That is not the question.

PAINE

It is precisely the question. If I am not a citizen, which of you became one first?

WARD

Step aside.

Paine does not.

Two CONSTABLES approach. Marguerite, waiting outside, sees the humiliation.

PAINE

The republic used my voice before deciding whether I had one.

He steps aside under his own power.

### INT. NEW ROCHELLE COTTAGE — NIGHT

Paine writes a protest, fury overcoming pain.

PAINE (V.O.)

The right of voting for representatives is the primary right by which other rights are protected.

His hand cramps. He forces it open.

PAINE (V.O.) (CONT’D)

To take away this right is to reduce a man to slavery.

The candle dies. His pen scratches once more in darkness.

### INT. NEW YORK PUBLIC HOUSE — EVENING

Mechanics and young reformers crowd around Paine. A broadside advertising his *AGRARIAN JUSTICE* lies beside the ale.

A YOUNG RADICAL raises his glass.

YOUNG RADICAL

To universal liberty.

The table cheers. Paine does not drink.

PAINE

How does the widow buy bread?

YOUNG RADICAL

What?

PAINE

Your universal liberty. Can she spend it at market?

YOUNG RADICAL

Once government is made free, prosperity follows.

PAINE

Government has been free for thirty years. Hunger remains punctual.

He pulls the broadside closer.

PAINE (CONT’D)

The earth was not created with title deeds. Civilization enclosed the common inheritance and made fortunes possible. Very well. Then property owes something to every person it excludes.

AN OLDER MECHANIC squints at the figures.

OLDER MECHANIC

Money at twenty-one. A pension after fifty.

PAINE

Not charity. Compensation.

YOUNG RADICAL

They will call that robbery.

PAINE

Men born to an estate consider every obligation robbery.

A WELL-DRESSED PATRON at the next table snorts.

PATRON

This from a gentleman given a farm by the state.

Paine turns.

PAINE

Precisely. I know what public generosity can do for a man. My objection is to its rarity.

The mechanics laugh. The patron leaves.

Paine finally lifts his glass, hand trembling. The Older Mechanic steadies the bottom without calling attention to it.

PAINE (CONT’D)

To liberty that survives the market.

They drink.

### INT. NEW YORK PRINT SHOP — DAY

An APPRENTICE, 16, sorts type backward. Paine watches.

APPRENTICE

Is it true you wrote *Common Sense*?

PAINE

The printer performed the heavier labor.

APPRENTICE

My master says it changed everything.

PAINE

Everything claims to have changed. Ask what remains the same.

The apprentice holds up a reversed metal “R.”

APPRENTICE

You read backward?

PAINE

Useful practice for politics.

The boy grins.

### EXT. NEW YORK STREET — DAY

Paine exits. The noise overwhelms him.

He stops, dizzy. People flow around the old man who once redirected history.

The apprentice runs out and catches his elbow.

PAINE

I do not need carrying.

APPRENTICE

Then we are walking in the same direction.

They walk to the corner together.

### INT. GREENWICH VILLAGE HOUSE — BEDCHAMBER — DAY — 1809

Back to the opening room.

Paine is weaker. Marguerite opens a window.

BENJAMIN BONNEVILLE, now a young man, sits among property papers.

PAINE

The farm goes to your mother. The south lot—

His breath catches.

MARGUERITE

Enough.

PAINE

Enough is a word executors use.

MARGUERITE

Enough is a word used by the woman holding the ink.

She caps the bottle.

Paine glares. Benjamin hides a smile.

### INT. GREENWICH VILLAGE HOUSE — HALL — AFTERNOON

Two MINISTERS stand wet from rain.

FIRST MINISTER

We understand Mr. Paine is near the end.

MARGUERITE

Then you understand he is tired.

SECOND MINISTER

The state of his soul cannot wait.

PAINE (O.S.)

Consolation rarely requires two witnesses.

Marguerite reluctantly opens the door.

### INT. BEDCHAMBER — CONTINUOUS

The ministers enter.

FIRST MINISTER

Mr. Paine, do you wish to make peace with Jesus Christ?

PAINE

I have no quarrel with your Jesus. It is his proprietors who trouble me.

SECOND MINISTER

Do you believe he is the Son of God?

PAINE

Must I spend my last breath satisfying your curiosity?

FIRST MINISTER

Your eternal life—

PAINE

Is not improved by a lie told under pressure.

The first minister kneels and begins praying aloud.

Paine turns toward the wall.

PAINE (CONT’D)

At this hour I remain the man I was at noon.

### INT. BEDCHAMBER — NIGHT

Rain taps the window.

Paine sleeps fitfully. Marguerite dozes in a chair.

PAINE

Close the door.

She wakes.

PAINE (CONT’D)

The mark. It is on the inside.

MARGUERITE

There is no mark, Thomas.

PAINE

Good.

He drifts.

PAINE (CONT’D)

Franklin has the bridge.

MARGUERITE

Yes.

PAINE

Tell Washington—

His anger cannot survive the sentence.

MARGUERITE

What shall I tell him?

PAINE

Nothing. Make him wait.

Even now, a flicker of mischief.

### INT. BEDCHAMBER — MORNING — JUNE 8, 1809

The rain has stopped.

A wedge of clear sky above the rooftops.

Marguerite holds the cup. Paine’s hand rests over hers.

PAINE

Open the window.

She does.

The sounds of New York enter: wheels, a hammer, a distant printer’s press.

Paine listens.

His fingers loosen.

The cup is entirely in Marguerite’s hand.

She waits.

No next breath.

A page stirs on the table.

### INT. NEW YORK NEWSPAPER OFFICE — DAY

An obituary is composed.

EDITOR

“Thomas Paine, author of *Common Sense* and the notorious *Age of Reason*, died yesterday—”

APPRENTICE

Notorious?

EDITOR

Readers expect balance.

APPRENTICE

Between independence and a book they dislike?

The editor takes the composing stick from him.

EDITOR

Between employment and unemployment.

### INT. QUAKER MEETINGHOUSE — DAY

Plain benches. Silence.

Marguerite faces TWO ELDERS.

MARGUERITE

His father was Quaker. He asked to be buried here.

FIRST ELDER

Thomas Paine was not in membership.

MARGUERITE

He asked for plain ground, not approval.

SECOND ELDER

We cannot receive him.

Silence returns, now revealed as a wall.

### EXT. ROAD TO NEW ROCHELLE — DAY

A small wagon carries the coffin.

Marguerite and her sons follow. Three other MOURNERS. Six in all.

They pass summer fields. Travelers overtake them without knowing.

### EXT. PAINE FARM, NEW ROCHELLE — DAY

A grave beneath a walnut tree beside the road.

Six mourners. No official. No preacher.

Wind moves the leaves.

Benjamin Bonneville looks down at the coffin.

BENJAMIN

Should someone say something?

The others look toward Marguerite.

She takes a folded page from her dress.

MARGUERITE

He has said enough.

She places the page on the coffin. We glimpse Paine’s handwriting.

Soil falls.

The sound becomes a PRESS SLAMMING.

### MONTAGE — THE PARTIAL MEMORY

— A schoolbook prints: “Thomas Paine, patriot author of *Common Sense*.”

— A teacher closes the book before *The Age of Reason*.

— British workers pass *Rights of Man* hand to hand.

— A woman speaker quotes Paine on generations’ freedom.

— An abolitionist circles “all mankind.”

— Rain wears the New Rochelle grave.

### EXT. PAINE GRAVE, NEW ROCHELLE — NIGHT — 1819

Lanterns emerge from darkness.

WILLIAM COBBETT, 56, commanding and restless, stands over the grave with TWO LABORERS.

One laborer hesitates.

LABORER

You are certain this is lawful?

COBBETT

Lawful is not the same as right. Mr. Paine taught me that.

LABORER

Knew him, did you?

Cobbett looks at the spade.

COBBETT

I slandered him.

The laborer waits.

COBBETT (CONT’D)

Dig.

### EXT. GRAVE — LATER

The coffin has collapsed. Lantern light touches a skull and bones among dark soil.

Cobbett kneels, shaken by the reality of his plan.

COBBETT

I called you everything they wished to hear.

He lifts the skull with both hands.

COBBETT (CONT’D)

Come home.

### EXT. ATLANTIC — SHIP — DAY

A wooden trunk sits lashed beneath canvas.

Gray sea. England ahead.

### INT. LONDON HALL — NIGHT

Cobbett addresses a thin gathering beside the trunk.

COBBETT

We will raise a monument worthy of the man. A national subscription—

An ORGANIZER whispers in his ear.

Cobbett looks at rows of empty chairs.

### MONTAGE — THE BONES DISAPPEAR

— The trunk moves beneath Cobbett’s desk.

— Years pass in papers and dust.

— Cobbett dies.

— An heir opens drawers, frowns at unlabeled fragments.

— A small box passes to a dealer.

— A claimed skull is measured.

— A claimed hand vanishes into a private cabinet.

— The empty New Rochelle grave beneath snow.

PAINE (V.O.)

It is error only, and not truth, that shrinks from inquiry.

### EXT. PAINE MONUMENT, NEW ROCHELLE — PRESENT DAY

Traffic passes the roadside monument.

No one stops.

Wind moves the grass over the empty grave.

### INT. PUBLIC LIBRARY — AFTERNOON — PRESENT DAY

Fingers move along book spines.

A NEW READER, 17, takes down *COMMON SENSE*. No costume, no emblem—just a young person curious enough to open a book.

At a table, the reader studies Paine’s portrait, then the first page.

NEW READER

(quietly)

“Society in every state is a blessing, but government, even in its best state, is but a necessary evil...”

The library sound falls away.

### SERIES OF IMAGES — THE WORDS FIND THEIR FIRST READERS AGAIN

— Young Thomas beneath the workbench, listening.

— The condemned thief on the scaffold.

— Franklin writing the letter.

— Ink rolled across type.

— The Black sailor hearing “all men.”

— Bloody footprints in New Jersey snow.

— The Young Soldier standing straighter.

— Workers crowding around *Rights of Man*.

— Louis XVI alone before the blade.

— Chalk on the inside of a prison door.

— Jefferson at a table while an enslaved hand pours wine.

— Paine refused at the ballot.

— Six mourners beneath the walnut tree.

— Cobbett holding bone in lantern light.

BACK TO LIBRARY:

The New Reader turns a page.

PAINE (V.O.)

A long habit of not thinking a thing wrong gives it a superficial appearance of being right.

Outside the library window: a demonstration moves down the street. Handmade signs. Police barriers. People arguing over what freedom requires now.

The reader looks from page to street.

Closes the book around one finger.

Then rises and carries it toward the door.

### EXT. PUBLIC LIBRARY — CONTINUOUS

The New Reader steps into late light.

The book opens again in the wind.

CLOSE ON the page. Black letters. Paper grain. A thumb in the margin.

The ink looks wet.

FADE OUT.
